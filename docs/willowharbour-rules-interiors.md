# Willowharbour interior placement rationale

The reference house separates room volumes with dark gaps and narrow connecting halls. Native wall tops wrap onto vertical sides; flooring changes at room boundaries. Furniture clusters express activities, and windows occupy usable wall space rather than appearing behind wardrobes. These principles guide the ten authored programmes in `packages/tools/src/willowharbour-interior-design.ts`.

| Building | Rooms and placement decisions |
| --- | --- |
| Garden cottage | Sitting room by the entrance, separate cooking/dining wing, private bedroom, tiled bathroom. Chairs face the table, bedside lighting stays within reach, mirror hangs above washstand. |
| Orchard cottage | Rustic kitchen and bedroom suit a working orchard household. A separate sitting room and tiled bath preserve domestic functions without duplicating the garden cottage's finish. |
| Inn | Two compact guest rooms each have one bed, storage and writing space. Central kitchen serves the shared dining hall; tables and nearby chairs leave the service route open. |
| Carpenter | Workbenches and benches form two practical work bays. An office holds the limited reference shelf, while finished dining furniture occupies the customer display room. |
| Furnisher | Coordinated sitting, sleeping and dining displays show furniture in use. The table lamp sits on a supporting table; rugs define display groups without filling every floor. |
| General store | Cabinets and barrel groups hold goods; counter space faces circulation. Staff writing/rest space remains separate. Bookcases and decorative chests are unnecessary here. |
| Greenhouse | Two planted soil beds flank a clear aisle, with mature ferns and flowering pots. Potting and washing fixtures sit at the working end. |
| Barn | Compact hay storage occupies one side; feed barrels and workbenches occupy the other. The central aisle leads directly to the exit and resident. |
| Guild | Two reference-library wings justify six bookcases in total, with reading desks immediately nearby. Separate office and sitting rooms support administration and conversation. |
| Smith | Sales and office rooms use timber finishes. The shared forge/work room uses stone, with hearths against the back wall and clear access around anvil and workbench. |

Placement constraints apply to every room: solid fixtures fit its floor envelope, door/service routes remain reachable, seats belong near their tables, mirrors project onto walls above washstands, windows sit on supported north walls, and tall storage leaves those windows visible. Plants occupy corners and grouping edges rather than door mouths. Distinctive shops get relevant inventories; bookcases appear only where books have a purpose. No random chests remain in these interiors or the Willowharbour exterior. Unrelated Cinder content and player storage are preserved.

The programme is authored rather than a random room generator. Interior scale is intentionally larger than each facade, while domestic, commercial and agricultural envelopes retain their different purposes.
